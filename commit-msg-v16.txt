payroll(us): add DC -- 49/50 states plus DC (v16)

DC's bracket table is high confidence: two independent sources (DC
OTR's own current "DC Individual and Fiduciary Income Tax Rates" page,
and a separately-fetched 2022 USDA NFC bulletin TAXES 22-28) landed on
the identical 7-row table. The NFC bulletin additionally supplied the
S/M/N/H filing-status categories and the $4,300-per-dependent
allowance. That allowance figure is only as current as its 2022
source -- no more recent DC bulletin was located -- flagged in
limitations for reconfirmation before real 2026 payroll runs, same
caveat pattern already used for VA/KS/ID/VT's dated bulletins. No
separate DC standard-deduction figure beyond the dependent allowance
was found in the sources obtained, so taxable wages here are annual
wages minus only the dependent allowance -- flagged as a possible gap
rather than assumed complete.

Wisconsin was investigated further this pass and deliberately NOT
closed. Its 2025 Form 1 annual-return instructions give the exact
top-bracket (5.3%/7.65%) formula and thresholds for taxable income at
or above $100,000, and its standard-deduction phase-out formula was
already confirmed self-consistent. But that worksheet is the ANNUAL
RETURN's own computation, not the payroll WITHHOLDING formula, and
gives no percentage-method formula for the lower two brackets (only a
$100-wide wage-bracket lookup table). Mixing annual-return constants
into a withholding calculation without confirming they apply the same
way was judged too risky to ship -- left unbuilt rather than guessed.

Full self-test suite (all v1-v16 cases) passes, including a new
hand-verified DC case (annual wages $120,000, 1 dependent, filing
status S: taxable $115,700 falls in the 8.5% bracket, annual tax
$8,234.50, monthly $686.21).
